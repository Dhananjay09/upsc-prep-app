KOEPPEN'S SCHEME OF CLASSIFICATION OF CLIMATE
Three broad approaches have been adopted for classifying climate: empirical (based on observed data, particularly temperature and precipitation), genetic (organises climates according to their causes), and applied (for specific purposes). The most widely used classification is the empirical scheme developed by V. Koeppen, who identified a close relationship between the distribution of vegetation and climate. Developed in 1918 and modified over time, Koeppen's scheme is still popular and in use. He selected values of temperature and precipitation, related them to vegetation distribution, and used capital and small letters to designate climatic groups and types.

Koeppen recognised five major climatic groups, four based on temperature and one on precipitation:
A - Tropical: average temperature of the coldest month is 18°C or higher
B - Dry Climates: potential evaporation exceeds precipitation
C - Warm Temperate (Mid-latitude): average temperature of the coldest month is higher than minus 3°C but below 18°C
D - Cold Snow Forest Climates: average temperature of the coldest month is minus 3°C or below
E - Cold Climates: average temperature for all months is below 10°C
H - Highland: cold due to elevation

Climatic groups are subdivided into types using small letters based on seasonality: f = no dry season, m = monsoon climate, w = winter dry season, s = summer dry season. Small letters a, b, c, d refer to degree of severity of temperature. B-Dry Climates are subdivided using capital S (steppe/semi-arid) and W (desert).

GROUP A: TROPICAL HUMID CLIMATES
Tropical humid climates exist between the Tropic of Cancer and Tropic of Capricorn. The sun being overhead throughout the year and the presence of the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) make the climate hot and humid. Annual range of temperature is very low and annual rainfall is high.

TROPICAL WET CLIMATE (Af) — directly corresponds to Goh Cheng Leong's "Hot, Wet Equatorial Climate"
Tropical wet climate is found near the equator. The major areas are the Amazon Basin in South America, western equatorial Africa, and the islands of the East Indies. Significant rainfall occurs in every month of the year, mostly as thunder showers in the afternoon. Temperature is uniformly high and the annual range of temperature is negligible. The maximum temperature on any day is around 30°C while the minimum is around 20°C. Tropical evergreen forests with dense canopy cover and large biodiversity are found in this climate.

Tropical Monsoon Climate (Am): found over the Indian sub-continent, North Eastern South America, and Northern Australia. Heavy rainfall mostly in summer; winter is dry.
Tropical Wet and Dry Climate (Aw): occurs north and south of Af regions, bordering BW/Cf climates; found north/south of the Amazon forest, Sudan, south-central Africa. Annual rainfall considerably less than Af/Am and variable; wet season shorter, dry season longer with more severe drought; temperature high throughout the year with greatest diurnal range in the dry season. Deciduous forest and tree-shredded grassland occur here.
