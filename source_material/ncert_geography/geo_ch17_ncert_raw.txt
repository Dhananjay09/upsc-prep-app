TROPICAL WET AND DRY CLIMATE (Aw) — directly corresponds to Goh Cheng Leong's "Savanna or Sudan Climate"
Tropical wet and dry climate occurs north and south of Af (Tropical Wet) climate regions. It borders BW (dry) climate on the western part of the continent and Cf or Cw climate on the eastern part. Extensive Aw climate is found to the north and south of the Amazon forest in Brazil and adjoining parts of Bolivia and Paraguay in South America, Sudan, and south of Central Africa. The annual rainfall in this climate is considerably less than in Af and Am climate types, and is also variable. The wet season is shorter and the dry season is longer, with the drought being more severe. Temperature is high throughout the year, and diurnal ranges of temperature are the greatest in the dry season. Deciduous forest and tree-shredded grassland occur in this climate — matching Goh Cheng Leong's description of savanna vegetation as deciduous, adapted to a long dry season, with tall grass and scattered trees.

KOEPPEN CLASSIFICATION CONTEXT (recap, for the small-letter/capital-letter notation used across the Tropical Humid Climates group)
Group A (Tropical) climates are divided into three types: (i) Af — Tropical wet climate (no dry season, used for Ch.15's Hot Wet Equatorial Climate); (ii) Am — Tropical monsoon climate (monsoonal, short dry season, used for Ch.16's Tropical Monsoon Climate); (iii) Aw — Tropical wet and dry climate (winter dry season, used here for the Savanna/Sudan Climate). The small letter 'w' specifically denotes a winter dry season in Koeppen's notation.
