COLD SNOW FOREST CLIMATES (Group D) — context
Cold snow forest climates occur in the large continental area of the northern hemisphere between 40°-70° north latitude, in Europe, Asia and North America. They are divided into two types: (i) Df — cold climate with humid winter; (ii) Dw — cold climate with dry winter. The severity of winter is more pronounced at higher latitudes.

COLD CLIMATE WITH DRY WINTERS (Dw) — directly corresponds to Goh Cheng Leong's "Cool Temperate Continental (Siberian) Climate"
Cold climate with dry winter occurs mainly over north-eastern Asia. The development of a pronounced winter anticyclone and its weakening in summer sets in a monsoon-like reversal of wind in this region — an interesting parallel to Goh Cheng Leong's own note that European U.S.S.R. and eastern Siberia receive extra moisture from Westerlies and the South-East Monsoon respectively. Poleward, temperatures are lower, and winter temperatures are extremely low, with many locations experiencing below-freezing-point temperatures for up to seven months in a year — matching Goh Cheng Leong's account of Siberian rivers (the Ob, Lena, Yenisey) remaining ice-covered for more than 210 days (7 months). Precipitation occurs in summer. The annual precipitation is low, from 12-15 cm (120-150 mm) — notably lower than Goh Cheng Leong's own figures for Moscow (533 mm) and Churchill (406 mm), since NCERT's Dw figure describes the driest, most continental interior parts of the Siberian climate belt specifically, consistent with Goh Cheng Leong's own observation that 'Central Siberia and Canada have about 380 mm... due to their continentality', with the amount decreasing further polewards/southwards to 305 mm or less.
