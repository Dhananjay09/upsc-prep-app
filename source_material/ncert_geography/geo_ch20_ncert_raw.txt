MID-LATITUDE STEPPE (BSk) AND MID-LATITUDE DESERT (BWk) — directly corresponds to Goh Cheng Leong's "Temperate Continental (Steppe) Climate"
Per Koeppen's classification (as tabulated by NCERT), Dry Climates (Group B) are divided into steppe/semi-arid (BS) and desert (BW) types, each further subdivided by latitude: subtropical steppe (BSh) and subtropical desert (BWh) occur at latitudes 15°-35° (already used for Ch.18's hot desert content); mid-latitude steppe (BSk) and mid-latitude desert (BWk) occur at latitudes 35°-60° — the latitude band matching Goh Cheng Leong's continental interior Steppes, Prairies, Pampas, Veld and Downs. NCERT's classification table defines Group B overall by the criterion that potential evaporation exceeds precipitation, consistent with Goh Cheng Leong's account of the Steppe climate's light, variable rainfall (508 mm average, ranging 254-762 mm) driven by continental remoteness from maritime moisture sources.

The small-letter suffix 'k' in Koeppen's notation denotes the mid-latitude (cooler) variant of the BS/BW climates, as distinguished from the 'h' suffix used for the (generally hotter) subtropical variant — directly paralleling Goh Cheng Leong's own observation that Eurasian Steppe stations like Winnipeg have a far greater annual temperature range (39°C) than subtropical/coastal desert stations, owing to their continental, higher-latitude position.
